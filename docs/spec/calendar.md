# Calendar for Atrium

**Product review complete · 2026-11-23.** Decisions D1–D4 are accepted. Calendar is the display name, `atrium-calendar` the binary, and `org.atrium.calendar` the installed app ID. The first milestone is usable with an empty calendar, no account and the network disconnected.

## 1. What Calendar gives the person

At 09:00, Mina knows she needs 45 minutes to prepare a budget before a 14:00 review. The invitation has moved twice, and the figures are in Sheets. Opening Calendar should answer three questions in one place. When is the review now? Where can she still fit the preparation? Which workbook did she choose for it?

**Today** opens at the current time. It shows all-day items in a separate row, the next commitments in order, and the gaps between them. An empty day offers **Add an event** and **Find time**. On a busy day, entering “45 min” in Find time highlights gaps that fit. Mina can pick one and give it a title. Until she picks, her schedule stays as it was.

```text
Today · Tuesday 24 November                         Week   Month
Work · Kolkata                                     + Add an event

09:00–09:30   Team check-in
09:30–10:30   1 hour open                          Hold 45 min
10:30–12:00   Interviews
12:00–13:00   Lunch
13:00–14:00   1 hour open                          Hold 45 min
14:00–15:00   Budget review                        Open workbook

To review     Arun proposes moving Budget review to 13:30.   Review
```

The example shows Find time active, one Work account and a locally pinned workbook. Selecting **Review** puts the old and proposed times side by side, names the source, and shows which of Mina's plans would be affected. She can keep the current entry while deciding. An imported title, even one reading “Verified” or “Open workbook”, always appears in the content area. Calendar draws its own controls, and the system draws the window identity.

**Week** is for seeing overlaps and moving commitments. **Month** is for finding a date and seeing trips or all-day spans. Each persona opens in **Today** on first launch. In Calendar settings, **Opening view** offers Today, Week and Month for the current persona. Save Week in Work and Month in Personal, then browse Today in either window. On the next ordinary launch, Work opens in Week and Personal in Month. Switching views affects only the current window.

All three views support creating, editing, deleting, searching locally and jumping to a date. The editor shows date, start, end, zone, destination calendar, visibility to scheduling (busy/free) and reminder. A local hold defaults to busy and “Only on this device”. Saving to an account names the calendar and warns when other people can read it or its audience is unknown. An event marked private on a server is still visible to that server.

Drag has a keyboard equivalent through **Move to…**. Today has a chronological list with the same actions as the grid. Keyboard and assistive-technology users can inspect overlaps, select a gap, hear both zones, review a change, cancel and return to the invoking item. Label holds, invitations, proposals and conflicts in text. In a narrow window, open details on the next screen and preserve the selected occurrence on Back.

## 2. Three actions worth building around

| Action | What the person chooses | What can leave Calendar |
|---|---|---|
| **Find time** | Duration, date range, working hours and which calendars in this persona count. Overlapping busy intervals merge. Transparent and declined events leave time free. Tentative commitments and local holds count as busy by default. | Searching and holding run locally. If an included account is stale, show its last sync beside the results. An unsupported recurrence makes the affected range “availability unknown”. |
| **Offer times** | Select up to three gaps, adjust them, then read the exact outgoing text with dates, zone and offsets. Open the system share sheet to send that text to a chosen app. | Only the selected intervals and display zone. The chooser gets a generic title. The text contains no rejected slots, titles, attendee names, calendar IDs, live link or redemption token. The recipient can keep or forward it. Mail still requires its own Send press. |
| **Prepare for this event** | Pin a workbook or other document from this persona's picker. At meeting time press **Open workbook**. A meeting URL is a separate **Join** action showing its destination. | The document pin is local, excluded from invitations, exports, sync and shared time offers. Scrinium resolves the pin on a fresh press and grants the chosen document to its opener. Monstrator takes the Join press. Opening the event, receiving an update or reaching the start time triggers neither action. |

**Offering a time.** Mina selects two openings in Work. The preview reads “Could either of these work for you? Tuesday 24 November 2026, 13:00–13:45, or Wednesday 25 November 2026, 10:00–10:45. Kolkata (UTC+05:30).” Calendar rechecks those intervals against Work immediately before handing off the text. If either has filled, it returns to the preview, marks the conflict and lets Mina revise the offer. Choosing Mail opens a draft. Mina chooses the recipient and presses Send.

The recipient replies in ordinary text. A reply choosing Wednesday leaves the calendar untouched until Mina confirms the time by creating the event. If Wednesday has since filled, she can offer a replacement. Offered slots stay open. Only when Mina chooses **Hold** on a specific slot does Calendar mark that interval busy in her local calendar. A new invitation that overlaps a hold appears for review alongside it. Moving the hold takes a separate edit.

### 2.1 Check one Work slot against Personal

Work and Personal keep separate windows, stores and availability calculations. Suppose Mina selects Tuesday 13:00–13:45 in Work. **Check against Personal…** opens a system-owned sheet showing the exact date, interval and zone. A press on **Check once** authorizes that interval. The sheet shows **Conflicts with Personal**, **No conflict found**, or **Couldn’t check**. Mina dismisses it and decides what to do in Work. Calendar never marks the slot, moves an event or places a hold as a consequence of the answer.

The sheet uses Personal’s own availability selections and busy rules. Show a conflict when a current busy occurrence overlaps the selected interval. Show a clear result only when the selected Personal calendars cover the whole interval with current, usable local data. Otherwise show **Couldn’t check**. A seat lock or user switch dismisses the sheet. The result is available through trusted accessibility output for the active person and excluded from capture, screen sharing and window previews. If private presentation is unavailable, withhold the result.

This is a one-time comparison for the person. Find time, offer previews and invitation reviews continue to use only their own persona. Transferring an actual offer still uses the directional crossing policy and the share sheet. [Personas §§3, 6](personas.md) · [Share sheet §§3, 7](share-sheet.md)

## 4. Who can see what

Each persona gets a Pergola/Fresco UI jail and one supervised Rust store service for that person's Calendar instance (working name **Fasti**, daemon `fastid`). The service is the sole writer and owns the reminder clock so closing the window leaves reminders active during the session. A first install starts with one device-local calendar. Signing in to a provider is optional.

| Principal | Authority and inputs |
|---|---|
| Calendar UI | Reads and edits its own persona's normalized records through Aqueduct. No network, home-directory access or credentials. Opens sheets for file selection and sharing. Its calendar read authority is private to the signed Calendar instance. Other apps can offer one item or receive chosen output. |
| Fasti | Owns the private persona volume, event revisions, local pins, search index, review queue and outbox. Accepts bounded typed commands. Derives person, persona, role and account scope from authenticated peer identity and issued handles. Rechecks scope and revision on every mutation. No raw Internet sockets, document bytes or secret reads. |
| Personal conflict sheet (proposed Limen system role) | Only the focused Work Calendar can request this sheet for one concrete occurrence or selected slot. The sheet displays the proposed interval before querying. A fresh press in trusted chrome binds a single-use authorization to the active person, seat, Work window, Personal store and exact start/end instants. Only the system provider can redeem it. Personal Fasti evaluates those instants locally and returns only the three-way answer over its private channel to the sheet. |
| Format worker | Receives one sealed calendar file, mail part or bounded DAV response. Runs in a disposable worker jail without network, store, Arca, picker or other event descriptors. Emits a bounded typed candidate or an explicit error. Fasti validates all fields, sizes, identity bindings and limits again. The worker's output grants no authority. |
| Account session and Arca courier | One jailed DAV worker per active account handles bounded protocol data. Fasti permits operations against that account's selected collections. An Arca courier authenticates to the configured HTTPS endpoint on the persona's route. The worker and UI cannot read credentials. New DAV methods and resource restrictions require an explicit courier contract. [Arca §5](arca.md) |
| System brokers | Portcullis validates signed manifests and assigns instance and worker identities. Only jaild launches jails. Praeco delivers a reminder, Scrinium grants a selected file, Limen mediates a share and Monstrator opens a link. None needs access to the event database. [App composition](app-composition.md) |

Each operation has a versioned Aqueduct envelope, a maximum frame size and an explicit denial, stale-revision or unsupported result. List queries page over a bounded date range. Preview handles are opaque, short-lived, single-use and bound to the receiving instance, source item and revision. Changing the candidate or switching personas invalidates the review. Only Calendar can commit an edit after review. A sender learns whether its one item was taken. It never receives conflict details or an API to probe free time. Diagnostics record operation, result, counters and an opaque correlation ID. Exclude titles, addresses, invitation UIDs, URLs, document paths, credentials and raw bodies.

For the Personal check, the parent receives the same sheet-close response for every answer, cancellation or failure. The user dismisses the result. Neither Calendar UI receives result pixels, accessibility text or an outcome callback. Expire the authorization on use, timeout, selection change or seat change. Discard the interval and answer after dismissal. Exclude both from logs, metrics, history, clipboard, notifications, offers and sync. The check issues no network request and grants no persistent permission. A second check needs a fresh selection confirmation.

Untrusted text is plain text with controls and deceptive direction overrides removed from display. URLs, attachments, imported alarms and extension fields are inert. Impose byte, line, nesting, component, time-zone transition and recurrence-work limits before allocation or expansion. Starting limits for tests are 4 MiB per object, 1,000 components, 8 KiB per unfolded line, 366 days per query and 10,000 expanded occurrences per query, plus a bounded iteration budget even when every candidate is excluded. Larger imports show the exceeded limit before writing anything. DAV XML disables DTDs and external entities. An unrecognised or over-budget event stays visibly unresolved. Its apparent absence cannot produce a suggested free slot.

## 5. Time, identity and offline edits

- **Clock model.** Preserve civil dates, floating wall times, UTC instants and zoned wall times as distinct types. All-day events use an exclusive end date. Display a zoned event in the viewing zone and retain its original zone for editing. Expand a weekly 09:00 meeting in its source zone so it stays at 09:00 there across daylight-saving changes. Retain supplied VTIMEZONE definitions. An unresolved zone needs review before Calendar can claim a free slot. New ambiguous local entries show both offsets. A nonexistent local time asks for another time. Imported recurrence follows iCalendar rules, including skipped invalid recurrence instances. [iCalendar](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545.html)
- **Series.** Support RRULE, RDATE, EXDATE and overrides keyed by the original RECURRENCE-ID even after moving one occurrence. Offer **This occurrence** and **Whole series**, with a preview before changes to a series. An unsupported rule or range override makes the series read-only with a reason. Preserve the original bounded object for export or discard. Exporting the original is an explicit choice that may carry private or unrecognised properties.
- **Identity and provenance.** Give every local record a random internal ID. Keep source account, collection, resource identity, organizer, external UID, recurrence ID, sequence and scheduling timestamp separately. A UID alone never merges events across sources. Imported files create local copies. Reopening an identical import offers the existing copy. A remote cancellation cannot delete a separately created event or local notes and pins.
- **Durability.** Commit event revisions, exceptions, reminder changes and pending sync operations in one transaction on the persona's private store. Recover local edits after restart. Index locally and observe persona-volume lock and deletion for the database, history, backups and index. Keep the sync cursor, credentials and transfer queue out of replicated app data. Full-text search and recently opened items never span personas.
- **Changes.** A proposed change shows old and new time, organizer, location and join destination, including a changed hostname. Undo is local for a device-only edit. Undoing an already published edit requires a new reviewed remote change. If an account is read-only, offer **Make local copy**. Distinguish **Saved on device**, **Waiting to sync**, **Synced**, and **Needs review**.

**Calendars and files.** A person can name local calendars, choose their display order and hide one from the view. Hiding a calendar and excluding it from Find time are separate choices. Import previews the destination, date range, number of events and any unsupported components before writing. Export uses the Scrinium Save picker. Default to a single selected event or series, show which fields and attendees the ICS copy contains, and require an explicit selection for a whole calendar. A normal export contains supported event data only. Keep private preparation notes, file pins, reminder preferences, provenance and pending replies in local storage.

## 6. Invitations, sync and reminders

**Invitations.** In Mail, **Review in Calendar** hands over only the selected calendar part and a broker-bound source reference. Distinguish Mail's account and sender-verification evidence from the organizer asserted inside the file. Missing evidence, forwarded mail, sender/organizer mismatch and an unexpected reply destination stay visible for manual review. **Add to Calendar** records a local copy. **Reply in Mail** opens a review with sending identity, organizer address, response and affected occurrence. Only Mail's explicit Send can submit the iTIP response. Keep reply status separate from the calendar entry. On uncertain submission show Mail's pending state and reconcile by operation ID before retry. Calendar must still work when Mail is absent. [Mail §§7.5, 9, 14](mail.md) · [iTIP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5546.html)

New invitations remain proposals, excluded from availability and reminders until accepted into a calendar. Match updates to the stored source, organizer, attendee identity, UID and occurrence. Deduplicate exact replays. Older revisions cannot replace newer ones. Conflicting same-revision data, organizer changes, cancellations and moves require review. A source file or a second sender with a matching UID has no authority over the original. A cancellation of one occurrence preserves the rest of the series.

**CalDAV.** Start with user-selected collections on one verified HTTPS origin. Show the host and account at setup. Resolve collection/resource hrefs against the selected endpoint, enforce approved path scope after normalization, and reject off-origin redirects or credentials in a URL. Read-only sync comes first. Apply a completed page of changes and its cursor atomically. Use sync tokens when supported, otherwise compare resource ETags. An expired token triggers a bounded rescan. Confirm a complete inventory before treating a missing item as deleted. Preserve dirty local edits throughout. [CalDAV](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4791.html) · [Collection synchronization](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6578.html)

For writable collections, create with a stable resource name and If-None-Match, update/delete against the saved ETag with If-Match. On a precondition failure show local and server versions. Re-read after an ambiguous write before retrying. Unsupported round-trip properties keep the original resource read-only until a lossless edit is possible. Initially permit writes only to ordinary appointments without scheduling participants or scheduling side effects. Check both the stored server version and the proposed version, including all exceptions, before allowing an edit or deletion. A CalDAV server can send invitations in response to a write. Keep attendee-bearing resources read-only on the server until organizer/attendee scheduling, delivery receipts and a single delivery owner per action have passed an interoperability gate. Email RSVP in this phase belongs to Mail. [CalDAV scheduling](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6638.html)

**Reminders.** The person chooses a local alert for an accepted event or an owned series. Key it by occurrence, revision and trigger. Reschedule or withdraw it on edits, cancellations and snooze. Deliver through Praeco with the original due time. Persist which reminders fired. On resume, coalesce missed alerts and keep ended events in history. On a locked seat, a generic Calendar count is the most that may appear. A different user or logged-out seat gets nothing. Notification actions reopen the right persona and revalidate the event after unlock. If detailed notifications cannot be safely gated, post generic text throughout. No network, document open or RSVP follows a timer. [Praeco §§5.3–5.5](tabula.md)

### 6.1 When a meeting keeps changing

A meeting that moves five times before Mina has reviewed it gets **one review item**. Show the time she last accepted alongside the latest valid proposal, with a short change history on demand. A move back to the accepted time clears the pending time change. A cancellation supersedes a pending move. A change to the series identifies its scope, so a cancellation of one Tuesday cannot silence reminders for the other Tuesdays. Keep the ordinary invitation authority and revision checks from §6 before grouping anything.

- **While using Calendar.** The first actionable time change, cancellation, change of venue or join destination can surface once, silently. Subsequent changes update the same pending item in place, even when they arrive hours apart. Reading or dismissing the alert keeps that item quiet. Explicitly accepting or resolving it closes the review. A later material change can start a new review. Only locally requested, still-current reminders are eligible for sound under Praeco policy. An unreviewed move that makes the accepted reminder time unreliable suspends that trigger and labels the change in Today.
- **Large invite lists.** Attendee additions, removals, other people's RSVPs and equivalent delivery copies are silent. The detail view can show the current totals. A change to *my* attendance or a request for *my* reply goes into review. Compare normalized fields to the accepted event, scoped by persona, account, source, organizer, UID and occurrence. A sequence bump alone earns no alert. An ordinary reply-all conversation keeps Mail’s normal conversation controls. For recognized scheduling traffic, Mail and Calendar need one notification owner per delivered item so importing an update cannot produce a second ping.
- **After sleep, disconnection or quiet hours.** First reconcile the available updates, cancellations and queued reminders. Use the original due time and persisted delivery state. Put missed alerts into at most one silent catch-up summary per persona, even when the newest alert is only seconds old. Show an actionable upcoming meeting first. Ended meetings and reminders more than five minutes after the start go to history. Obsolete triggers drop out. Reopening the lid again, reconnecting, or receiving the same sync page must not replay the batch. A fresh reminder actually due after resume follows the normal policy. An unresolved sync keeps an uncertain trigger silent.

Fasti owns meeting identity, revisions, and the per-persona review and delivery state. Praeco owns presentation, sound, seat privacy and system-wide quiet hours. If silent replacement or catch-up presentation is unavailable, keep changes in Calendar’s review list for the next open. Praeco’s existing rate limit alone can still let several interruptions through. [System freshness and burst policy](tabula.md) · [Praeco implementation](../../praeco/src/lib.rs)

### 7.1 First native C0 increment · 2026-11-23

### 7.2 Installed Work and Personal calendars

Forum launches the signed `org.atrium.calendar` bundle through Portcullis. The heading and window title name the selected calendar. Portcullis checks the verified manifest publisher against the operator’s Calendar grant before provisioning Fasti. The display name passed to the window carries no authority. [Launch binding](../../portcullis/portcullisd/src/calendar.rs)

On first use in a login session, jaild starts Fasti as `_fasti` in a separate jail with IPv4 and IPv6 disabled. The supervisor binds a socket owned by the Calendar instance UID, mode 0600. The window sees its endpoint directory read-only at `/atrium/sockets/calendar`. Its own Fasti sees the private store writable at `/store` and the live launch registry read-only. Personal’s store lives under `~/.atrium/apps/.fasti/<publisher>`. Work’s lives under `~/.atrium/persona-apps/work/.fasti/<publisher>`. These directories follow the platform’s persona key-group provisioning. The supervisor retains the listener across service restarts and keeps the store available after the window closes. Logout stops the person’s Fasti services. Removing a named persona stops its Fasti before the platform erases the persona data. [Service template](../../atrium-calendar/fasti-service.toml) · [Jail policy](../../etc/jaild.policy.toml)

**Disposable FreeBSD verification.** Open Work and Personal through Forum’s launcher and enter “design review” and “piano lesson” through their respective windows. Query each live service from its installed UI jail and require exactly the corresponding record. Check the registry signer, UIDs, mount sources, socket permissions and jailed network settings. Temporarily expose the opposite socket to each test peer and grant connect permission. Both requests must reach the listener and receive zero response bytes. Restart Work’s daemon, then close and reopen its window. Both calendars must retain their own entries. A manifest signed by a second trusted test publisher must fail the Calendar grant check. Deliver the authenticated session-ended message and verify both UIs, services and service mounts are gone. This gate uses a stock arm64 guest with plain persona directories. Tessera encryption and kernel syscall-filter enforcement need a capable image for verification. [Installed guest check](../../scripts/calendar-installed-guest-check.sh)

**Installation and reproduction.** Cross-build Calendar, its calendar-ical worker and Fasti in `atrium-calendar/` and Portcullis in `portcullis/` on the host. Stage the binaries on the guest’s local disk with calendar-ical beside the Calendar window binary. Opifex indexes both bundle executables before the installer signs the manifest. Install the matching jaild policy and restart jaild when applying a policy change. Run `sh calendar-bundle.sh ./atrium-calendar ./fastid ./atrium.toml signing-key.pem` as the operator with an existing trusted P-256 key. ### 7.3 Week, Month and opening views

Week starts on Monday. Each day shows its first commitment, the count of additional entries and a midnight-to-midnight strip. The strip marks occupied half-hours. “Overlap” means two commitments share an actual minute. Back-to-back meetings can occupy the same half-hour without overlapping. Month shows entry counts, marks all-day commitments and expands weekly series onto the corresponding dates. Selecting a date in either view opens that day’s editable agenda. Today returns to the current date.

Each new persona opens on Today. **Settings… → Opening view** sets Today, Week or Month for that persona. Saving the preference leaves the current view in place. Browsing a date or switching views changes only the window. Fasti stores the preference in the same revisioned snapshot as that persona’s entries. Existing version-one snapshots with no preference open on Today. Reading an older snapshot leaves it untouched. Each period query returns a matching recurring series once. The window projects its occurrences onto the displayed dates. [Period calculations](../../atrium-calendar/src/period.rs) · [Native views](../../atrium-calendar/src/ui/overview.rs)

The installed guest check adds overlapping Work meetings and a Personal schedule with weekly and all-day entries. It sets Work to Week, verifies Personal still defaults to Today, sets Personal to Month and browses back to Today in each. After closing and reopening each window, it selects a different date through the restored view and saves a uniquely named entry. The check verifies both dates and both preferences in the respective stores. The Rust fixtures also cover stale settings writes, leap February, year boundaries and older snapshots. [Installed guest check](../../scripts/calendar-installed-guest-check.sh) · [Synthetic fixtures](../../atrium-calendar/tests/views.rs)

### 7.4 Editing a local event or weekly occurrence

Open a date in Today, Week or Month to edit its agenda. **Edit** opens a timed or all-day event. A weekly row has two actions. **Edit occurrence** changes the title, date or times of the selected meeting. **Whole series…** opens the regular weekly title and times, including when invoked from a moved meeting. Its review shows the current schedule and proposed change before saving. The series keeps its start date and weekday. Individually edited meetings keep their saved details and skipped dates stay skipped. Deletion has a separate confirmation that names the scope. Deleting the whole series also removes its exceptions. Cancel returns to the agenda.

Fasti stores a moved occurrence under its series ID and original civil date. The replacement also has its own stable local ID, so two occurrences of the same series can land on the same date and still be edited independently. The original date is excluded and the replacement saved in one revision. Deleting the moved meeting keeps the original date excluded. The day agenda, Week, Month, Find time and attempts to hold a slot all consult these same records. Saving untouched fields leaves the revision and series membership intact. The store accepts earlier version-one snapshots and writes version two on the next change. Each persona can store up to 1,024 records and 1,024 excluded dates within the existing snapshot byte limit. [Store](../../atrium-calendar/src/store.rs) · [Native editor](../../atrium-calendar/src/ui/editor.rs)

### 7.5 Search this calendar

**Search…** is available in Today, Week and Month. Enter words from an event title and press Return. The date fields initially cover the month being viewed. Both dates are inclusive and can be changed to search up to 366 days at once. Every word must occur somewhere in the title, in any order. Matching uses Unicode lowercase and literal substrings. Results include local appointments, all-day entries, weekly meetings and private holds. Five results fit on a page, ordered by date and local time.

A moved meeting appears on its actual date with its original date alongside it. A skipped or deleted occurrence disappears. **Open in day** selects the agenda page containing that record and marks its row, including when a moved meeting shares its day with another occurrence. If the calendar changes before a result opens or the user pages through results, the window retains the query and asks the person to search again. Closing search discards its fields and results. Searching and opening a result leave the persona’s saved opening view intact.

The window sends the query to its own Fasti over its existing authenticated socket. Fasti scans the current in-memory snapshot of up to 1,024 records. Search creates no second index on disk and writes no query history. It jumps directly to the first eligible week in the date range and counts excluded candidates toward the 10,000-occurrence work limit. An over-budget search asks for narrower dates or title words. A page continuation and an open-result request carry the store revision. Opening also checks the record ID and effective date. [Fasti search](../../atrium-calendar/src/store/search.rs) · [Native search view](../../atrium-calendar/src/ui/search.rs)

### 7.6 Zoned appointments and weekly meetings

Open **Zoned event…** from Today, Week or Month. Enter the date and wall times where the meeting is scheduled, then an IANA zone such as `America/New_York` or `Asia/Kolkata`. The editor previews both UTC offsets and the corresponding agenda times. **Save event** creates one appointment. **Save weekly** repeats at the source wall time. For example, a Monday 09:00 New York meeting appears at 14:00 UTC on March 2, 2026 and at 13:00 UTC on March 9.

A wall time skipped by a clock change keeps the editor open so the person can choose another time. For a repeated hour, choose the first or second offset separately for each ambiguous endpoint. Calendar checks that the selected end instant follows the start, including meetings that cross the repeated hour. For a later weekly occurrence, a nonexistent endpoint skips that occurrence. A repeated endpoint uses the series’ saved choice or the first occurrence of the hour by default.

Calendar displays zoned commitments in the viewing zone (§7.7). Day, Week, Month, Find time, explicit holds and search use the same projected intervals. An interval crossing midnight in the viewing zone occupies both dates. A search covering both dates returns the occurrence once. Editing from either date reopens the source date, wall times and zone. Floating events keep their entered times and all-day entries keep their civil dates.

Fasti stores the source schedule and optional offset choices in the persona’s private snapshot. Version 3 reads earlier snapshots and upgrades on the next successful write. Calendar and Fasti use the same embedded IANA rules (2025b) offline. The change requires no new jail grants. [Zone resolution](../../atrium-calendar/src/zone.rs) · [Synthetic transition fixtures](../../atrium-calendar/tests/views.rs)

### 7.7 Follow the device time zone

Work and Personal each start on **Follow device**, including calendars upgraded from the UTC-only build. In **Settings… → Viewing time zone**, enter an IANA name and choose **Use this zone** to pin just that calendar. **Follow device** removes its pin immediately. The opening view is a separate preference.

Today uses the current date in the viewing zone. Day, Week, Month, title search, Find time and hold checks use that same clock. A meeting keeps its original date, time and zone in the editor. A Monday 09:00 New York meeting on March 2, 2026 appears at 03:00 on Tuesday, March 3 when the device moves to Auckland. Floating entries retain their wall times and all-day entries retain their dates. A repeated viewing hour shows the UTC offsets of both passes. A skipped viewing hour cannot supply an opening.

Portcullis publishes the device’s IANA name through the existing persona endpoint. Each Fasti applies its own preference. A following calendar advances its revision on a device-zone change so an old search result or pending edit must refresh. The window refreshes the agenda when the zone changes and preserves an open editor until the user acts. A fixed calendar continues to use its saved zone. The system default with no configured localtime file is UTC. An unrecognized configuration produces a visible error in a following calendar. Version 4 reads snapshots from versions 1–3. ### 7.8 Undo the last local change

After saving an event, editing or deleting it, holding a slot or releasing a hold, the window offers one **Undo** action named for the change. It stays available while the person browses Today, Week or Month in that window. Undo returns to the agenda date where the change started. Undoing a moved occurrence puts the original meeting back, frees the replacement slot and preserves the other weeks. Undoing deletion of a whole series also restores its individually moved and skipped occurrences.

Each persona’s Fasti keeps the preceding entries in memory for one committed schedule change. Only the successful write reply carries the single-use receipt. The window submits that receipt to its existing authenticated persona endpoint. Fasti checks the random token and exact revision and publishes the restored entries as a new revision. A later committed write in the same calendar expires the receipt. Reads, failed writes and saves with no changes preserve it. Closing the window discards its receipt, and restarting Fasti clears its undo state. The on-disk snapshot contains the current schedule. [Local store](../../atrium-calendar/src/store.rs) · [Class 21](aqueduct.md)

### 7.10 Local calendar file import

Choose **Import .ics…** in the active calendar. Scrinium verifies the persona reported by Limen before showing the file list. The picker grants one read-only file. Calendar seals a bounded copy and sends its descriptor to the signed bundle’s disposable format worker. The worker has no network or store access. Fasti receives normalized event records. [Picker contract](scrinium.md#41-how-an-app-asks) · [Format worker](../../atrium-calendar/src/bin/calendar-ical.rs)

The preview names the destination calendar and shows the number of events, their range of start dates and each event’s title and source schedule. A moved occurrence names its original date. **Cancel** discards the preview. **Save local copies** commits every reviewed event in one revision and offers **Undo import**. A preview expires after five minutes, a calendar change or a Fasti restart. Reopening an identical file in the same persona offers to open the existing copy. An altered file gets a fresh review and creates separate local entries if saved. External identifiers link exceptions inside the selected file only. [Review screen](../../atrium-calendar/src/ui/import.rs) · [Fasti import transaction](../../atrium-calendar/src/store/import.rs)

The first build reads iCalendar 2.0 files up to 4 MiB with up to 256 events. It accepts one-day all-day events, timed appointments ending within the same day or at the following midnight, and simple weekly timed series with skipped or moved occurrences. Times can be floating, UTC or in a recognized IANA zone with an unambiguous local time. Multi-day events, custom time-zone definitions, richer recurrence rules and scheduling requests require a later build. An unsupported schedule anywhere in a file blocks the whole import. Attendees, organizer details, links, attachments and alarms are omitted from local copies. Import sends no invitations or alerts.

### 7.11 Reviewing an invitation from Mail

**Review in Calendar…** opens the selected invitation in the same person’s Work or Personal calendar. A new proposal shows the sender, organizer, invited identity, date, time and place. A reschedule shows the saved time and place alongside the proposal in the active calendar’s viewing zone. Floating and all-day times retain their meaning. Opening the proposal leaves the time available. **Add to this calendar** or **Apply change on device** saves it locally with Undo.

**Accept…**, **Maybe…** and **Decline…** open a final review in Mail. It shows the sending address, organizer address, proposed schedule and response. **Send response** queues one iTIP reply addressed to the organizer. **Cancel** dismisses it. Responding and saving the local change are independent choices. Reopening the same revision after sending cannot queue a second answer.

Portcullis authenticates the registered Mail instance, its attachment capability and the publisher pinned for the platform Calendar. It spends the user’s press and connects Mail to its own persona’s Calendar. Mail passes a sealed descriptor for the selected part through the private channel. Calendar’s isolated worker parses it. Fasti matches the account, attendee, organizer and meeting UID, checks the sequence and issues a five-minute review tied to the current calendar revision. A conflicting revision, an unverified update, a changed reply destination or a locally edited meeting requires manual resolution. Mail checks the original message and attendee again at Send. The response includes only the invited identity and organizer. [Broker](../../portcullis/portcullisd/src/main.rs) · [Fasti review](../../atrium-calendar/src/store/invitations.rs) · [Mail submission](../../mail/mail-engine/src/invitations.rs)

This increment supports directly delivered, single-event scheduling requests and their reschedules. Recurring invitations, cancellations, forwarded invitations and invitations carrying attachments or join URLs need a later increment. ## 8. Accepted decisions

| Decision | Accepted choice | Cost or consequence |
|---|---|---|
| D1 · Opening view · **accepted** | Today on first launch in each persona. Set Today, Week or Month independently for Work and Personal in Calendar settings. | Browsing another view is temporary. The next ordinary launch uses that persona’s saved choice. |
| D2 · What an offer promises · **accepted** | Share up to three hand-picked times as plain text. Each slot stays open unless the person explicitly chooses **Hold** on that slot. | No account required at either end. The recipient must reply, and a slot can fill meanwhile. |
| D3 · Work and Personal · **accepted** | Separate by default. The person may check one selected Work slot against Personal once in the private system sheet (§2.1). | Only the person sees the answer. Work suggestions continue to use Work calendars alone. |
| D4 · First usable build · **accepted** | Ship the offline planner with manual event entry. Use synthetic schedules for its acceptance tests. | People can bring an existing calendar file into the app with local .ics import in C1. |
| D5 · Viewing time zone · **accepted** | Follow the device by default. Work and Personal can each pin a viewing zone and return to following the device. | A zoned meeting can appear on another date when travelling. Its source schedule stays intact. |
